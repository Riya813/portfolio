What I discovered

The product was invisible in search. It was optimised for "secret messenger", a phrase with almost no demand, while the high-volume "calculator vault" term belonged to photo-hiding apps with different intent.

It also turned out to be an app-store game: people find privacy apps through Play Store listings and roundup articles, not brand websites.

What I discovered

Generic terms like "buy plants online" belong to big plant retailers. But preserved flowers and floral jewellery get comparable search volume with far less competition, and preserved flower bottles were already one of the brand's top Instagram reels.

Buyers also search "mybageecha reviews" before purchasing, and the third-party review profiles were unclaimed with no brand replies.

What I discovered

Speed and links were fine. The page competed with specialist security firms on generic "VAPT services" terms, used the same copy as every competitor, had no VAPT-specific proof, and was orphaned from the site's strong cloud pages.

What I changed

Personalised marketing & GDPRConsumer survey research What makes people trust brands with their data. Survey
Research question

How do consumers balance the value of personalisation against data privacy under GDPR?

Method

Designed a survey covering GDPR awareness, privacy invasion, opt-outs and trust.

Sample

Respondents across age, gender, education, employment and tech familiarity.

Key finding

Clear compliance statements and explicit consent raise trust and keep people opted in.

What it means for marketers

Explain, don't buryPlain-language consent and compliance statements build more trust than long policies.
Give controlOpt-outs often follow one invasive message. Let people tune what they get.
Meet them where they learnPeople learn their rights from forums, news and social media, so privacy messaging belongs there too.
